<TextTwoColsNRows
  content="<b>[13] Card Design</b>
  The card design is based on the research about PwD's sensitivity to touching and pictures' arousal of their old memories. The design combines multi-sensory elements: (1) a picture (vision) related to PwD's memories and (2) the fabric (touch) matching the contents of the picture. In this way, PwD can have multiple methods of finding the match. The memory training through the game can also be strengthened.

  <b>[14] Board Design</b>
  A big piece of cardboard is used as the physical low-fidelity prototype for the playing board of the game. The top right corner of the cardboard has a standing clamping slot, mimicking the &quot;scanner&quot; of the cards. The shadow areas represent the &quot;slots&quot; where cards are placed.
  
  <b>[15] Overall Prototype</b>
  The final prototype is shown with cards placed on the playing board, together with the tablet with the media-playing user interface in front of the playing board. Players will use the cards on top to play a normal “flip-flop match game”. Once the match is found, the card can be scanned by the scanner and the simulated television will showcase audios or videos corresponding to the card contents."

  <b>[23] A Metaphor</b>
  To simulate a normal activity: “card inserting”, which is common in various daily situations like retrieving money in the ATM, entering/exiting a parking lot, and so on. A set of cards can represent a complete story, e.g., “A trip to Italy with my daughter Amy, wearing my flowered T-shirt and carrying my Polaroid camera.” In this way, once all the matches are found, the “story” is completed as a whole and the “memories” are retrieved."

<TextTwoColsNRows
  content="<b>[24] Battle Mode (PwD in Early / Middle Stage)</b>
  The hinge structure of the board enables a physical connection with another board, This allows two players to play against each other by flipping card sets on their respective board, which is a bit more challenging but adds to the gamification. Besides, by enlarging the number of cards, this mode underlines our notion of “sharing the memories”.
  
  <b>[25] Normal Mode (PwD in Middle Stage)</b>
  Using one board, this mode allows PwD to play the game with caregivers/family members, who will lead the PwD to press the button to examine the hidden card and touch the fabric on the wheel that corresponds to the content of the exposed card. Once a match is found, the retro TV display connected to the board via Bluetooth will play the corresponding video clip.

  <b>[26] Leisure Mode (PwD in Late Stage)</b>
  This mode simulates a normal activity: “card inserting”, which is common in various daily situations like retrieving money in the ATM, entering/exiting a parking lot, and so on. Easily understandable, the “insert and play the video” gaming circle serves as a process of “simply enjoying wonderful moments in life” and/or “weaving pieces of memories together”."

<TextTwoColsNRows
  content="<b>Battle Mode (PwD in Early / Middle Stage)</b>
  1. Caregivers arrange specific time and locations for two PwD to play the game together.
  2. Players take turns to play the memory game, with their board connected with each other’s.
  3. Whoever completes finding all the matches wins the game.
  4. The other player will also continue the game. In the end, both PwDs enjoy the whole process of admiring both their own and their “opponent’s” memories.
  
  <b>Normal Mode (PwD in Middle Stage) </b>
  1. Caregivers or family members arrange specific time and locations for one PwD to play the game.
  2. Under the guidance of the caregiver or a family member, the player flips the card to find the match.
  3. The matched cards trigger the playing of the corresponding video in the retro-TV display.
  4. Once all the matches are found, the player “wins” the game, with the retro-TV display showing a complete story.
  
  <b>Leisure Mode (PwD in Late Stage)</b>
  1. Caregivers or family members arrange specific time and locations for one PwD to play the game.
  2. The caregiver or family member shows the card set and explains how to play with the cards.
  3. Under the guidance of the caregiver or a family member, the player chooses the card to insert into the retro-TV display.
  4. The display will play the corresponding video clip to the selected card.
