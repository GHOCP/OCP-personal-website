<TextTwoColsNRows 
  content="This study explored human back’s haptic perception when two tactile illusions were applied: the Phantom Tactile Sensation (localization) and Tactile Apparent Motion (direction perception). The results indicated challenges in localizing the vibrations on participants' backs, with answers dispersed over a large area. As for direction perception, participants had a better perception of diagonal directions. When the phantom vibrations were used, participants could also perceive the direction correctly, especially in specific directions. The study contributed to the application of the above-mentioned illusion into creating tangible user interfaces especially for people with sensory impairments (e.g., deaf-blindness)."

<TextTwoColsNRows 
  content="<b>[2] Illusion 1: Phantom Tactile Sensation</b>
  When two vibrators, which are placed closely on the skin, are simultaneously actuated, an illusory vibration will be perceived. When their vibration intensities are the same, the phantom vibration will be generated in the midpoint of these two vibrators.
  
  <b>[3] Illusion 2: Tactile Apparent Motion</b>
  When two actuators are placed on the skin at a certain distance and vibrate in sequence with overlapped vibration time, a movement across the skin between the actuators will be perceived (SOA: Inter-stimulus Onset Asynchrony)."

<TextTwoColsNRows 
  content="The experimental apparatus consisted of a laptop computer, nine coin-style eccentric rotating mass (ERM) motors, and an Arduino Mega microcontroller. Besides, a chair with adjustable backrest height, and a pair of headphones were also used. Nine coin-style ERM motors were placed on the backrest of a chair forming a 3 × 3 equally spaced (2.5 centimeters) square grid.
  
  During the experiment, the participant sat straightly and naturally on the chair with a backrest cover, under which the vibrator array was glued to the backrest. The motor array was placed on the lower left side of the backrest. There was a distance of 3 cm between the leftmost border of the moor array and the center vertical line of the backrest. The participant was told to overlap her/his spine with the center vertical line of the backrests. The height of the chair’s backrest was always adjusted to align the center motor with the participant’s navel."

<TextTwoColsNRows 
  content="<b>Testing localization for Phantom Tactile Sensation</b>
  For localization test for Phantom Tactile Sensation, 8 different pairs of motors were switched on. A stimulus consisted of two motors vibrating simultaneously for 0.3 s. All 8 pairs were presented five times in blocked random order. After each trial, the participant spoke out the number of the perceived vibration location on a five-by-five dot grid (printed on a sheet given to her/ him). The experimenter logged the answer into another computer."

<TextTwoColsNRows 
  content="<b>Testing direction perception</b>
  All eight different directions that went through the center motor were presented in random orders. Three vibrations were given sequentially, each lasting for 0.3 seconds. Once a vibration was finished, the next would be given instantly (i.e., the onset between two sequential vibrations could be regarded as 0.3 seconds).
  
  The center vibration was generated with 5 different ways. It could be by the physical motor placed on the spot. It could also be a phantom vibration created by two appropriate motors."
